1. Momentum and impulse in a crash ≈ 12 min

When a car hits a solid barrier it has to stop. All of its momentum has to be removed — and the barrier does that by pushing back on the car with a force. How big is that force?

Key idea. The momentum of a moving object is its mass × velocity. When a force acts on an object for a time, it changes the object's momentum. Force × time is called the impulse, and it is equal to the change in momentum, Δp.
p = m v      impulse = F Δt = Δp      so   F = Δp ÷ Δt
Impulse: the change in momentum of an object, equal to force × the time the force acts. Unit: N s (the same as kg m/s). In this workbook we only use the size of the impulse and force, so you can ignore any minus signs.
Typing numbers: type the number only (the unit is shown next to the box). You can type big numbers as 1800000, 1,800,000 or in standard form as 1.8 x 10^6.
Worked example 1.1

An 800 kg car travelling at 12 m/s hits a wall and stops in 0.050 s. Find the force on the car.

Answer:
Momentum before: p = m v = 800 × 12 = 9600 kg m/s. Momentum after: 0 (the car has stopped).
Impulse: Δp = 9600 − 0 = 9600 N s
Force: F = Δp ÷ Δt = 9600 ÷ 0.050

2. The crumple zone test track ≈ 18 min

A crumple zone is a part of the car (usually the front and back) designed to squash in a controlled way during a crash. The longer the crumple zone, the further the car travels while it is stopping, so the stop takes longer.

3. Protecting the person inside ≈ 15 min

A crumple zone slows the car down. But what injures people is the force on the person. By Newton's first law, when the car stops the driver keeps moving at the car's old speed. They keep going until something — a seat belt, an airbag or the steering wheel — exerts a force on them and stops them.

Key idea. The person also has to lose all their momentum. Their Δp is fixed by their mass and speed, so the way to protect them is the same as before: make their stopping time Δt as long as possible, so the force F = Δp ÷ Δt on them is as small as possible.
17A 70 kg crash-test dummy is in the car at 10 m/s. What is the dummy's change in momentum when it is brought to a stop?
kg m/s
Δp = m v − 0, using the dummy's mass this time, not the car's.

Other safety features

Modern cars have many safety features. Read the cards, then answer the questions below.

Seat belt

Holds you to the car, so you slow down with it while the crumple zone squashes, instead of flying forwards and stopping suddenly on the dashboard. The belt webbing is designed to stretch a little in a crash.

Airbag

Inflates in about 0.03 s, then lets gas escape through small vents as your head and chest sink into it. It also spreads the force over a large area of your body.

Collapsible steering column

The steering column is made of tubes that slide into each other. If the driver hits the wheel, it moves forwards instead of acting like a rigid post.

Padded dashboard and soft trim

Soft, squashy surfaces where knees and heads might hit, instead of hard plastic and metal.

Rigid safety cell

The passenger compartment is a strong steel cage designed not to crumple. It keeps a space around the people inside so they are not crushed.

Automatic emergency braking (AEB)

Sensors spot an obstacle ahead and brake before the driver can react, so the car hits at a lower speed — or doesn't hit at all.

5. Extension: crash test lab fast finishers

This lab lets you design the whole car. In a real crash many things happen at once: the crumple zone squashes, the safety cell may start to crush if it is too weak, and the dummy moves forwards into the seat belt and airbag. The lab works all of this out and tells you the maximum force on the dummy.

What the settings mean.
  • Crumple zone rigidity (kN) — the force the crumple zone pushes back with while it squashes. Low = soft, high = stiff.
  • Safety cell rigidity (kN) — the force needed to crush the passenger compartment.
  • Seat belt stiffness (kN/m) — how hard the belt pulls back for each metre it stretches.
  • Airbag rigidity (kN) — the force the airbag pushes back with as the dummy sinks into it.
